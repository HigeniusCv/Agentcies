# Minyanim, Post-Belief — The Fourth Case

*Extends `weapons-grade-landscape.md`. That document ran three cases (Rajneeshpuram 1983, Secular Solstice, ZOE/Cluny 2026) against an epidemiological spine and parked a thesis: transmission mode is the master variable, and virulence, stability, and credulity are its downstream readouts. This document adds the case that was left out, which turns out to be the one that tests the thesis hardest — the East Bay alternative minyanim — and then takes up the question the landscape deferred: whether a sect can pound sand hard enough to bring its Deus in.*

---

## 0. Why this case is the hard one

The landscape's spine was Ewald's transmission–virulence tradeoff. Horizontally transmitted parasites evolve toward virulence; vertically transmitted ones get domesticated into mutualists, because killing the host kills your own vector. Rajneesh was the acute horizontal infection. Cluny was the endemic vertical symbiont. Solstice was a horizontal infection trying to go vertical and not yet managing it.

The East Bay minyanim break the frame in a productive way, because they are the case the tradeoff does not predict. They are **maximally mutualist and structurally sterile**. The host benefit is not in question — Or Zarua is your spiritual home; the liturgy is more intelligent than what the movements produce; the people are, by your own account, brilliant. There is no parasitism here to detect. And there is also, by your own account, no next generation. Aquarian's hippies hardly had families and are now too old to pray. OZ has no new blood, so everybody is your age. Nishmat Shalom carries a liturgy from Somerville that Somerville itself has not managed to hand down. Dafna is the newest kid and the question is open.

That combination — benign, beloved, and not transmitting — is not something the Ewald frame generates on its own. Ewald says vertical transmission *produces* benignity. He does not say benignity produces vertical transmission, and the minyanim are the proof that it doesn't. So the thesis has to be sharpened:

> **The vertical channel is infrastructure, not merit.** It is built out of buildings, schedules, salaries, schools, and cemeteries. A meme complex does not acquire it by being good for its hosts, by being true, or by being beautiful. It acquires it by someone paying for the building.

That is the amendment this fourth case forces, and it is worth more than the case itself. It also explains why Cluny, whose theology you find least defensible of the four, is the one with the best actuarial prospects — a point the landscape reached and this document can now say mechanistically rather than by analogy.

Before the arithmetic, though, the thing the arithmetic will not show. What the minyanim do, nothing else in the ecosystem does. They are where the liturgy actually gets rewritten rather than voted on by a movement committee; where a service is led by whoever can lead it; where people who were shown the door by institutions with excellent transmission records found a room with a door that opened. Keep that fixed, because §2 is going to be unsentimental and it should be read as a description of a mechanism, not a verdict on a community.

## 1. The four cases, restated

| | Rajneeshpuram | Solstice / Lighthaven / Mox | ZOE / Cluny | East Bay minyanim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transmission | horizontal, sterile | horizontal, aspiring vertical | vertical, pronatal | horizontal intake, vertical aspiration, neither achieved |
| Host benefit | negative (parasitic) | weakly positive | positive (mutualist) | strongly positive |
| Outcome | acute burn, 4 years | pre-critical | endemic | **hybrid vigor, infertile** |

The fourth column wants a handle and the honest one is **the mule**. A mule is what you get from a cross that produces genuine hybrid vigor — stronger, more surefooted, better tempered than either parent — and cannot reproduce. Counterculture × rabbinic tradition gave the havurah movement exactly that: liturgical and intellectual vigor neither parent could manage alone, in a form with no gametes. Every generation of mules has to be bred fresh from horses and donkeys. There is no lineage of mules.

That is a description of a structure, not an insult to it. Mules were the backbone of American agriculture for a century.

## 2. Why the virtues *are* the sterility

This is the analytical core. Every founding virtue of the alternative minyan is, read as a transmission mechanism, a cut vertical channel. The list is not a critique from outside; it is the movement's own charter, run backwards.

**Cohort capture is the master mechanism.** Or Zarua began gathering in spring 2004. If it was founded by a friendship network of people then in their forties, it is a community of people in their sixties in 2026 — and it is that regardless of anyone's theology, household structure, or effort. Friendship networks are age-homophilous; a community that recruits through friendship recruits its founders' cohort and then ages as a solid block with no intake at the bottom. Aquarian ran the same process one generation earlier: a 1974 cohort formed around Zalman's Berkeley Kabbalah workshop, aged together, and is now, in your phrase, too old to pray. This single mechanism explains both without appeal to anything else, and it is measurable — the diagnostic is the *variance* of the age distribution, not its mean. A healthy vertical community has a flat or bottom-heavy age pyramid. A cohort-captured one has a bulge that migrates rightward at exactly one year per year.

**No clergy means no one whose job is the children.** Or Zarua has no paid staff and no clergy; prayer and activities are led and organized by member volunteers. This is the movement's proudest feature and the one Kaunfer built a book around. It is also the reason there is no one in the room whose salary depends on there being a room in twenty years. Professionalization is not a corruption of voluntarism; it is the specific thing you purchase when you want continuity, and it is expensive precisely because continuity is.

**No building means no address and no classroom.** Or Zarua davens in a Friends Meeting Hall — which, given where the ZOE persona went, is a gorgeous accident: a Reconstructionist havurah praying in a Quaker meeting house is about as close to a living Collegiant as North America offers. It is also a rented room. Minyan Dafna meets upstairs at Congregation Netivot Shalom. Buildings are boring, they consume the money that would otherwise fund liturgy and social action, and they are the vertical channel. A child is enculturated in a place.

**Monthly cadence is the decisive one.** Or Zarua's Shabbat shacharit is the first Saturday of the month. Twelve exposures a year. Minyan Dafna is twice monthly, roughly twenty-four. Set that against daily Mass — the ZOE hardcore filled that chapel to overflow at an hour when the rest of the resort was asleep — or against thrice-daily davening in the Orthodox community four miles away. Ritual competence is a motor skill. It is acquired at the frequency motor skills are acquired, and twelve repetitions a year does not do it in any domain. A child raised at Or Zarua does not become a person who can daven; a child raised at Beth Israel does, whatever either child later believes.

**Adult intentionality selects for horizontal recruits, by design and on purpose.** The havurah movement's founding grievance was the child-centered suburban synagogue — the Hebrew school, the bar mitzvah factory, Judaism as pediatrics. Prell's and Weissler's ethnographies both put the anti-institutional, anti-pediatric impulse at the center of what these communities were *for*. So the founding meme is explicitly anti-vertical: it defines the good community as the one adults chose rather than the one children were processed through. It then discovers, one generation on, that "the one children were processed through" was a description of the transmission mechanism.

**A connoisseurship barrier screens for a cohort rather than a lineage.** Iannaccone's result — strict churches are strong, because costly requirements screen out free riders and raise club-good quality — is one of the load-bearing findings in the landscape, and it appears to apply here: the minyanim are demanding, and Sosis and Bressler's commune-survival data say demandingness predicts longevity. But the *currency* of the cost matters, and this is a real refinement of Iannaccone rather than an application of him. Sacrifice-and-stigma costs (dress, diet, Sabbath restriction, endogamy) are payable by anyone willing, and crucially they are payable *by children raised in them* — a child raised keeping kosher finds kashrut cheap. Cultural-capital costs are different. When the entry fee is liturgical sophistication, political fluency, and knowing the room, you have built a screen that a devoted newcomer cannot pay and that does not become cheaper by being raised inside it. That produces exactly what you observed and named: weirdly clique-y. Clique-iness is not a personality failure of the members. It is what a taste-denominated entry fee looks like from the doorway.

**Household structure is a minor amplifier, not the mechanism** — and it is worth being precise here because the imprecise version is both unkind and wrong. A congregation whose leadership and membership skew toward household forms with lower aggregate fertility has a thinner vertical channel in the aggregate; that is arithmetic about a channel, not a claim about any person, and queer Jewish families in the East Bay have children deliberately and often at considerable expense. But this factor is doing far less work than cohort capture, and conflating them gets the diagnosis wrong. OZ's problem, in your own words, is that everybody is your age. That is a network-topology fact about how the community recruits, and it would hold if every member were a straight couple with four kids.

**And the generous point, which is not a consolation prize.** The minyanim recruit horizontally because horizontal recruitment *is the service they provide*. They exist in significant part to catch people the vertical institutions dropped — women who wanted to lead, queer Jews, the intermarried, converts, the theologically unwilling, anyone for whom the transmitting institution transmitted rejection. An institution optimized for catching the dropped cannot simultaneously be optimized for not dropping people, because the population it serves is by definition not its own children. This is a genuine tradeoff between two goods, not a failure to achieve one good. The mule does work the horse cannot do.

## 3. The natural experiment inside five miles

The best feature of this case is that the control condition is local, contemporaneous, and demographically observable. Within a short bike ride:

- **Aquarian Minyan** (1974, out of Zalman's Berkeley workshop) — the West Coast Renewal original, cohort-captured, aged out.
- **Kehilla** (1984, Piedmont/Oakland, from Burt Jacobson's vision, David Cooper as rabbi 1999–2017, now emeritus) — the one that *institutionalized*: building, rabbi, staff, largest Renewal organization on the West Coast. The exception that identifies the variable.
- **Nishmat Shalom** — the Somerville liturgy transplanted, via Havurat Shalom (1968, Arthur Green with Zalman's backing) and its degendered *Siddur Birkat Shalom*, whose Hebrew grammar is better than anything the movements have shipped.
- **Or Zarua** (2004) — monthly, volunteer-led, no clergy, Quaker hall.
- **Minyan Dafna** — traditional-egalitarian, no rabbi, twice monthly, nested inside Netivot Shalom's building, plus a monthly co-hosted Kabbalat Shabbat and catered dinner.
- **Congregation Netivot Shalom, Beth Israel, Chabad** — building, school, clergy, weekly-to-daily cadence.

**The hard datum is Tehiyah.** Founded 1979 in the peak havurah generation, egalitarian, K–8, El Cerrito. Enrollment as high as 250 in recent years, 140 by 2018, 74 registered for the coming fall; a $1.9 million deficit; board voted unanimously to close on 17 July 2018. That is the vertical infrastructure for precisely this population failing in precisely this geography inside your own membership tenure. The board's stated reasons — housing costs, improving public schools, commute times — are proximate and probably true. The distal account is that liberal-Jewish households in the East Bay do not produce enough children to fill a K–8, and route the ones they produce elsewhere. Oakland Hebrew Day School and the Orthodox institutions did not close.

**The arithmetic, done explicitly, because it is the whole story.** Pew 2020: Orthodox fertility 3.3 children per woman against 1.4 for non-Orthodox. Median age 35 Orthodox, 53 Reform, 62 Conservative. Children per household 2.0 Orthodox, 0.5 Reform, 0.3 Conservative. Retention of those raised in the branch: 67% Orthodox, 65% Reform — *essentially identical*, which is the part everyone gets wrong. The continuity discourse has spent forty years on retention, and retention is not where the action is.

Take a crude per-generation cohort ratio of (TFR ÷ 2) × retention:

- Non-Orthodox: (1.4 ÷ 2) × 0.65 = **0.455**
- Orthodox: (3.3 ÷ 2) × 0.67 = **1.106**

Over three generations (roughly 75 years, so: your grandparents to your grandchildren):

- Non-Orthodox: 0.455³ = **0.094**
- Orthodox: 1.106³ = **1.351**

A fourteen-fold swing, driven almost entirely by fertility, with retention contributing almost nothing because it is the same on both sides. This is not a projection and it is not demography-as-destiny polemic; it is multiplication, and it is why the minyan question cannot be answered by making the minyan better. A more beautiful service does not appear anywhere in that expression.

**Caveats, stated plainly because this is your old trade.** The model ignores in-migration, denominational switching, intermarried households raising Jewish children, and conversion. Pew's retention figure tracks *denominational* identity, not Jewish identity, so it undercounts people who stay Jewish and move branches — which is a real flow and it runs toward the minyanim, not away from them. TFR is period, not cohort. Three generations of constant rates is a fiction. And the East Bay is a migration destination, which is exactly how a cohort-captured minyan can stay the same size for twenty years while transmitting nothing: it is being topped up laterally by arrivals, not from below by children. That last point is important — **flat membership is not evidence against sterility, and it is the most likely thing to have masked this for two decades.**

**Provenance discount, applied evenhandedly.** The best demographic work on independent minyanim is the 2007 National Spiritual Communities Study — 1,354 respondents, participants concentrated in their twenties and thirties, heavily "groomed not bloomed" (about 40% day school), seeking warm community first and meaningful worship second, spreading through social networks rather than institutions. Its lead author is Steven M. Cohen, forced out of Hebrew Union College in 2018 after eight women came forward and a Title IX investigation. Three historians subsequently argued in *The Forward* that the misconduct and the substance of his continuity-crisis research program were connected — that a body of work directing Jewish women toward early marriage and childbearing had an author with a pattern. The landscape applied a Bayesian haircut to Gino's ritual studies for the same class of reason and it would be incoherent not to apply one here. So: the minyan demographics are directionally consistent with everything else in this section and should not be leaned on alone. *(I was unable to retrieve the study PDF or the contemporaneous coverage directly — both 403'd — so these figures come at one remove through secondary summaries. Treat the percentages as approximate and the age skew as the only robust claim.)*

**The constructive note, and the testable prediction.** Minyan Dafna has done the one structurally different thing available: it nested inside a building that has a school, a Conservative congregation, and a weekly cadence, and it co-hosts rather than competes. That is a minyan that has outsourced its capital infrastructure and kept its liturgical autonomy — which is, in Simon's near-decomposability terms, exactly the right modularity. The prediction: **Dafna is the one of the five with a vertical channel, and not because of anything about its davening.** If in 2046 there are twenty-somethings at Dafna who were children there, the mechanism will have been the shared building and the twice-monthly floor, not the quality of the service. That is falsifiable and you will live to see it.

## 4. Feldman, and what davening does when the function is empty

First the record, corrected once and then dropped: it is **Noah** Feldman, not Judah. Maimonides School in Brookline, Harvard, Rhodes to Oxford, and the JD is **Yale** — Harvard Law is where he holds the chair, which is probably the source of the compression. The public fall you are remembering is the 2007 *New York Times Magazine* essay "Orthodox Paradox," about being airbrushed out of the Maimonides reunion photograph for showing up with a non-Jewish girlfriend. He married her. The book is *To Be a Jew Today* (2024).

Feldman's taxonomy is four patterns rather than four denominations: **Traditionalist** (law as eternal, rabbinic authority channels divine will), **Progressive** (God's truth as consonant with evolving moral progress), **Evolutionist** (law is binding *and* changes through human interpretation over time — the position he treats as the most intellectually strained), and **Godless** (Jewish identity and full practice without theistic belief, Judaism as civilization and inheritance rather than covenant).

Your description of where he lands — observing faithfully while holding the 613 to be man-made, no more divinely sourced than Monopoly — is the **Godless** category, and you are right that it is not the Recon rabbit trick. Kaplan's move was to keep the word *God* and redefine the referent as "the Power that makes for salvation," which lets you say the liturgy's sentences without lying. Feldman's Godless Jew declines that consolation and keeps the practice anyway. That is the harder position and it is the one your question is actually about: what staying power does davening have when the ritual has a recognizably empty function?

**Rappaport dissolves the premise, and he is the load-bearing instrument here.** Ritual, in *Ritual and Religion in the Making of Humanity*, is "the performance of more or less invariant sequences of formal acts and utterances not entirely encoded by the performers" — and his central claim is that **public, outward acceptance of the liturgical order is sufficient to enact it, even where that acceptance is devoid of personal commitment.** Acceptance is a public act. It is not belief, it never was belief, and Rappaport's point is that it is *better* than belief precisely because language permits lying about inner states and performance does not. Obligation is entailed by acceptance; breach of obligation is per se a breach. So the function is not empty. It was never doing the job Feldman is worried it has stopped doing. Davening was never a belief-transmission device; it is an acceptance-and-obligation device, and it works identically whether or not anyone in the room is a theist.

**Leibowitz is the sharper contrast and makes Feldman look less radical than he is usually taken to be.** Leibowitz had already insisted that observance must be *lishmah* — for its own sake, with no reason, no meaning, no experiential payoff, no human need served. On his account, giving a human purpose for the mitzvot is not a friendly liberalization but idolatry, the worship of man in place of God. That is a far more austere position than Feldman's, and it is Orthodox. Feldman's Godless observance is, structurally, **Leibowitz with the commander deleted** — the same refusal of instrumental justification, the same insistence on the categorical character of the obligation, minus the one who commands. Which sets up the only interesting question: the commander was clearly not load-bearing for *justification*, since Leibowitz already refused all justification. Was it load-bearing for *transmission*?

**A note on the category itself.** "Post-belief" presupposes that belief was the load-bearing membership criterion that has now lapsed, and there is a serious argument — Wilfred Cantwell Smith's *Belief and History*, Lopez's "Belief" in *Critical Terms for Religious Studies* — that this framing is a Christian import into the comparative study of religion, and a late one. Rabbinic Judaism is substantially orthopraxic; there is no creed to lapse from, no moment of assent that constitutes membership, and Maimonides' thirteen principles are conspicuous partly for how little institutional machinery ever enforced them. Rosenzweig, asked whether he laid tefillin, is supposed to have answered "not yet" — a reply that is unintelligible if belief is the gate and perfectly clear if practice is. This is contested and I would not lean the whole argument on it, but it means "post-belief Judaism" may name a transition that structurally never occurred. The thing that lapsed was not belief. It was the schedule.

**Which is the answer, and it unifies this section with §2.** Rappaport establishes that ritual works without belief *for the performer*. It does not follow that it transmits without belief *to the next performer*, and the whole Feldman question sits in that gap. Here is the claim, and it is falsifiable:

> **The heritable unit is the calendar, not the creed.** What a child inherits is not the parent's reasons but the parent's schedule. Belief is not transmitted by being held; practice is transmitted by being scheduled. And the characteristic move of the sophisticated post-belief Jew is to retain the reasons — to keep thinking about it, keep reading about it, keep the intellectual seriousness fully intact — while relaxing the cadence, because cadence is the part the reasons said was arbitrary.

Feldman's own practice is reportedly serious and frequent, so as an individual the position is stable; that is his book's demonstration and it is convincing. But the position generates, as a population, communities that meet monthly. Or Zarua's first Saturday is the same phenomenon as Feldman's argument, one level up. Twelve times a year is what the calendar looks like after you have correctly established that no one is commanding you to show up more often.

And this is what the commander was for. Not to justify the practice — Leibowitz was right that it justifies nothing — but to make the schedule non-negotiable. A schedule you can revise is a schedule that relaxes toward the mean of its members' enthusiasm, and enthusiasm is not heritable. That is why Cluny's people were in that chapel at dawn and it is why the fifteen-fold arithmetic in §3 runs the way it does. The daily office is a transmission technology, and it is one that post-belief observance has no mechanism to keep wound.

## 5. Bringing the Deus in — a vocabulary that isn't Land's

You're right to drop the word. It's Land's coinage in circulation, it carries an accelerationist freight that does no analytical work, and it sits one step from the basilisk. On that: park it in a sentence. Roko's thing is an artifact of a particular decision theory about acausal trade between an agent and its own future — it is a claim about rational choice under exotic assumptions, not a claim about how ideas propagate through populations. Different question, no bearing on anything here, and the reason it eats conversations is that it is a Boyer-perfect minimally counterintuitive concept: one ontological violation bolted to an otherwise ordinary frame, maximally sticky, epistemically worthless. Note that and move on.

The phenomenon you actually want a word for is real and has better-credentialed names. Ranked by how much weight each will bear:

**Autocatalytic set** (Eigen's hypercycle, 1971; Kauffman) — a collection of entities in which the production of each is catalyzed by another member, so that the set as a whole catalyzes its own production. Closure is the threshold: below it the thing decays, above it it sustains. This is the literal chemistry of bootstrapping, it is quantitative, it is the standard frame in origin-of-life work, and it carries no baggage. **If you want one replacement word, this is it** — and it reframes the sect question usefully: not "how hard are they pounding?" but "is the catalytic set closed?" A community whose ritual produces members who produce ritual has closure. A community whose ritual produces satisfied attendees does not.

**Barnesian performativity** (MacKenzie, *An Engine, Not a Camera*, 2006) — the measured case. Black-Scholes did not merely describe options markets; its adoption made prices conform to it. Economics as engine rather than camera. This is the empirical gold standard for a theory bringing about its own truth conditions, and — decisively — **MacKenzie states the limit himself.** The claim is *not* that any formula, proposed by sufficiently authoritative people, could have made itself true. A formula that led its users to lose money, or that opened unconstrained arbitrage against them, would have ceased to hold sway. Reality retained a veto; performativity operated only inside the envelope where it wasn't punished. That caveat is the governor on this entire question and you should keep it attached to the concept wherever you use it.

**Looping effects of human kinds** (Hacking, 1995) — classifications change the people classified, who then change the classification, which changes them. The rigorous philosophical version, developed on real cases (multiple personality, child abuse, homosexuality). Where Land gestures, Hacking specifies the feedback path.

**Self-fulfilling prophecy / the Thomas theorem** (Merton 1948; W.I. and Dorothy Thomas 1928) — "if men define situations as real, they are real in their consequences." The bank run is the canonical case and it is genuinely load-bearing. Worth noting that this survived the replication crisis while its close cousin did not: Rosenthal's Pygmalion expectancy effects are weak and contested, so cite Merton on institutions, not Rosenthal on classrooms.

**Searle's status functions and collective acceptance** (*The Construction of Social Reality*, 1995) — money, marriage, borders, corporations. Entities with no physical existence, constituted entirely by collective acceptance, possessing entirely real causal powers. **This is where the Deus actually can arrive**, and it is Durkheim's claim — that the god a society worships is the society itself, apprehended — with the metaphysics stripped out and a mechanism installed.

**Strange loop / Drawing Hands** (Hofstadter, *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, 1979) — the Escher image you reached for is already Hofstadter's canonical example of exactly this structure, and has been since before Land was writing. You have been citing the good version by accident; you may as well cite it on purpose.

**Egregore** — the occult term, out of Éliphas Lévi and the French nineteenth century, for a thought-form sustained by and acting back upon the collective that maintains it. Predates Land by a hundred years, names the same phenomenon, and carries a numinous register that the autocatalysis language does not. Use where you want the shiver.

**Münchhausen's ponytail** — keep it, but keep it as the *negative* case. It names the version that is genuinely impossible: no internal force lifts a system against gravity. Its value is as the boundary marker separating it from the versions that work, all of which turn out to involve an external substrate the metaphor conceals. The Baron had no ground; the sect has other people.

**Na'aseh v'nishma** (Exodus 24:7) — "we will do and we will hear." Practice first, understanding after; the acceptance precedes and produces the comprehension. The tradition already contains the bootstrap doctrine and puts it at Sinai as the founding formula, which is a considerably better provenance than a 1990s Warwick seminar.

**Pascal's *abêtissez-vous* → Althusser's materiality of ideology** — Pascal's advice to the man who cannot believe: do not try to believe. Take the holy water, have the masses said, kneel, move your lips. Belief follows the body. Althusser picks this up directly in the ISA essay (1970): ideology has a material existence in practices and apparatuses, and the subject's ideas are the practices he performs. This is the mechanism by which ritual manufactures the belief it was supposed to express — and it is the exact inverse of the Feldman worry in §4, which makes the pair worth running together. **Also worth noting**: the ZOE workout coach's line — *nothing can change your mind faster than moving your muscles, except the Holy Spirit* — is Pascal in gym clothes, delivered at 7am by someone who almost certainly had not read him. That is the doctrine surviving in the practitioner class after it has left the seminar, which is itself a datum about which memes transmit.

### The disciplined answer to your question

Can an extremely far-from-equilibrium sect pound sand so totally that they bring their Deus in? Three parts, and they come apart cleanly:

**(a) A Searlean god: yes, always, and almost trivially.** Collective acceptance constitutes an institutional fact with entirely real causal powers. It moves money, schedules bodies, makes and dissolves marriages, buries the dead, and determines what a child eats for eighteen years. That is not a lesser or metaphorical god; it is the same ontological category as the dollar, and no one thinks the dollar is imaginary. Durkheim said the society is what gets worshipped; Searle supplies the mechanism.

Which produces the second inversion, and it is at the expense of your own priors. **Kaplan's rabbit trick is the sober answer, not the dodge.** "The Power that makes for salvation" is not a euphemism deployed to let atheists say the Amidah without wincing — it is a substantially correct Durkheimian description of what the referent of the liturgy's second person has always been, and it is the one theology among the four cases that survives contact with the instruments in this document without amendment. The landscape's first inversion was that the domesticated meme outlasts the enriched one. This is the second: the theology you find embarrassing is the one that is true, and it is being held by the community least equipped to transmit it.

**(b) A god with powers exceeding the aggregate of its adherents: no.** MacKenzie's governor applies. Performativity works only inside the envelope where reality declines to punish the belief; a formula that loses money stops holding sway no matter how authoritative its sponsors. Sand-pounding does not add mass, and the far-from-equilibrium condition is not an advantage here — far-from-equilibrium systems are, by definition, the ones paying the highest maintenance cost to stay where they are. Rajneeshpuram was extremely far from equilibrium. It lasted four years, and the letter you wrote in 1983 correctly identified the reason before the collapse: the checkpoints and the body searches were the energy bill.

**(c) The exception, which is the one that matters, and it is yours.** All of the above assumes the Deus is a social fact. It changes completely when **the Deus is buildable**. Belief in a buildable god funds the compute that builds it, and then the loop closes for real — not Barnesian, not metaphorical, not an institutional fact, but an actual optimizing artifact that exists because enough capital was convinced it would exist. Run the four cases against that test:

- Rajneesh's Bhagwan was a man with twenty Rolls-Royces. Mortal, and the loop opened when he did.
- Cluny's God is two thousand years old, is not being summoned, and is explicitly held to require nothing from its adherents to exist. Their project is transmission, not summoning — which is why they are the ones with the school.
- Or Zarua's God is, on the best available reading, the community's own functioning correctly named. Real, Searlean, and bounded by the community's size — which is the problem §2 and §3 describe.
- **Lighthaven, Mox, and the Solstice are the only ones whose god is under construction.** Their Deus is the sole member of the set that can be brought in by being believed in, because belief in it is a capital allocation and the thing is made of capital.

So the framework's verdict on your own stated orientation is that the fear is correctly calibrated and pointed at the right one of the four. The etiolated filk is not the tell. The sect whose ritual is thin, whose liturgy is embarrassing, and whose god does not exist yet is the only sect in the comparison running a loop that can actually close — and the thinness of the ritual is not evidence against this, because unlike the other three, that community's transmission problem is irrelevant to its success condition. **Cluny needs grandchildren. Lighthaven needs one working artifact.** A movement that does not need to reproduce is not constrained by the arithmetic in §3, which is why it is the one case in this document where "weapons-grade" is not a metaphor drawn from epidemiology.

## 6. The dial table, five cases

| Dial | Rajneesh | Solstice / Lighthaven | ZOE / Cluny | **East Bay minyanim** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transmission (Ewald) | horizontal, sterile | horizontal → aspiring vertical | vertical, pronatal | **horizontal intake, no vertical channel** |
| Host benefit | parasitic, acute | pre-critical, benign-weak | mutualist | **mutualist, high** |
| Charisma (Weber) | founder-bound, un-routinized | no founder, manufacturing rite | routinizing on purpose | **deliberately refused** |
| Explore/exploit (March) | explore, no exploit | explore-heavy | exploit inherited capital | **exploit inherited capital, refuse the infrastructure** |
| Ritual cost currency (Iannaccone) | obedience + assets | attendance | sacrifice + stigma | **cultural capital — screens cohort, not lineage** |
| Cadence (the heritable unit) | total immersion | annual | daily to weekly | **monthly** |
| Capital infrastructure | commune (seized) | campus (owned) | hotels, chapels, B-school | **rented rooms** |
| Age structure | 20s–30s, replaced laterally | 20s–30s, replaced laterally | full pyramid | **single cohort, migrating right** |
| Whose god could arrive | dead | **buildable** | already arrived | Searlean, bounded by membership |

Two things fall out that are worth more than the table.

**First**, cadence and capital are the only two rows that predict the outcome, and they are the two rows that have nothing to do with the content of the belief. Every row that a participant would consider the important part — what is believed, how good the liturgy is, whether the theology survives scrutiny — is inert. This is the same result the landscape got with credulity: the belief's truth is not the variable, its transmission apparatus is.

**Second**, the minyan column is the only one where every cell is a deliberate choice made by intelligent people for defensible reasons. Rajneesh's sterility was a side effect of predation. Solstice's thinness is inexperience. Cluny's advantage is inheritance nobody alive earned. The minyanim looked at clergy, buildings, dues, schools, and weekly obligation, understood exactly what those things cost, and declined them on principle — which is why this is the only case in the set that is genuinely tragic rather than merely instructive.

---

## 7. Parked, with falsification conditions

The thesis this case adds to the landscape:

> **Transmission mode is not chosen at the level of belief; it is purchased at the level of infrastructure, and the unit that is actually heritable is the calendar.** Creed is downstream, negotiable, and — as Rappaport shows — not required for the ritual to function at all. Cadence is upstream and is the thing that either survives a generation or doesn't. Post-belief observance is therefore individually stable and intergenerationally fragile, not because the reasons are bad but because correct reasons about the arbitrariness of the schedule are precisely what relaxes the schedule.

What would falsify it, in descending order of how soon you could check:

1. **A cohort-captured minyan that recovers a bottom-heavy age pyramid without acquiring a building, a professional, or a weekly cadence.** Predicted not to exist. One clear case kills the infrastructure claim.
2. **Minyan Dafna in 2046.** If it has twenty-somethings who were children there, the thesis predicts the mechanism will be Netivot Shalom's building and school, not Dafna's liturgy. If it retains a generation while remaining twice-monthly and rootless, the cadence claim is wrong.
3. **Hadar.** The one independent minyan that built a yeshiva. If its participants' children are practicing at Hadar-descended institutions in twenty years, that is the infrastructure claim confirmed by the exception; if the yeshiva turns out not to have mattered, it is disconfirmed.
4. **Feldman's own children.** Crude, and none of anyone's business, but it is the actual experiment his book runs and he knows it.

Two things to collect that you are unusually well placed to collect, and that no one else is bothering with:

**The age histogram.** Not the mean — the full distribution, for each of the five East Bay communities, plus Beth Israel and Chabad as controls. Cohort capture has an unmistakable signature (a narrow bulge migrating right at one year per year) that is trivially distinguishable from healthy replacement (flat or bottom-heavy) and from lateral top-up (flat mean, high turnover, no children). You have the survey chops, the access, and the standing to ask. Nobody has done this for the alternative minyanim because the movements fund retention studies and retention, per §3, is not where the action is.

**The cadence census.** Annual service-hours per member, per community, weighted by how many of those hours a child could be present for. That single number is the thesis's independent variable, it is public information, and the ratio between Or Zarua and Beth Israel is going to be somewhere around thirty to one.

And one thing not to do. This document has an unmistakable implication — that saving your spiritual home means buying it a building and meeting every week — which is exactly the pediatric-institutional Judaism the founders of these communities left, at some cost, on purpose. The analysis says what the mechanism is. It does not say the mechanism is worth the price, and the people who declined to pay it were not confused about the bill. A mule is a good animal. It just isn't a breeding population, and the mistake would be discovering the arithmetic at 63 and concluding that forty years of the thing you actually loved were a demographic error.
